The law in \cref{eq:law} is not only a test; it is free supervision. Following the
self-consistency idea of Zhong \etal~\cite{zhong2023equivariance}, but with a metric target and a
frozen teacher, for a pixels-only
model $\hat D_\theta$ we keep a frozen copy $\hat D_0$ (the teacher) and minimise, on
unlabeled images $I$,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
  \mathcal{L} = {} & \big\| \mathcal{U}_b \log \hat D_\theta(\mathcal{Z}_b I) - \log \hat D_0(I)\big|_b \big\|_1 \\
  & + \lambda\, \big\| \log \hat D_\theta(I) - \log \hat D_0(I)\big\|_1 ,
\end{aligned}
  \label{eq:eft}
\end{equation}
with a zoom term (first) and an anchor (second), where $b$ is a random box with zoom factor $s\sim\operatorname{LogUniform}[1,3]$ and random
centre, $\mathcal{U}_b$ resamples the zoomed prediction onto the crop grid, and
$\cdot|_b$ crops. The teacher's prediction on the \emph{unzoomed} image is the target for
the zoomed one, so the student has to discover from the pixels that a zoom happened and
undo the size-based shortcut. The anchor keeps the student on the teacher for ordinary images.
No depth labels and no intrinsics are used.

\paragraph{Setup.}
We tune Depth Anything V2 metric Small (24.8M parameters), the outdoor head on the frames of
seven KITTI raw drives that are disjoint from the 13 drives of the KITTI val selection (3{,}323
images; the kernel asserts the disjointness), and the indoor head on the RGB of the 795
official NYUv2 training images. AdamW, learning rate $5\cdot10^{-5}$ (head) and
$5\cdot10^{-6}$ (backbone), batch 4, 3{,}000 steps, $\lambda=1$, horizontal flips, input height
392\,px, mixed precision on one T4 GPU. DIODE is never used for training: it measures whether the
model learned what a zoom looks like in general or only in KITTI and NYUv2. Frozen and tuned
models are evaluated with identical preprocessing.

\paragraph{The resolution shortcut and EFT+B.}
A digital zoom does two things at once: it narrows the field of view and it removes detail
(each output pixel is interpolated from fewer input pixels). A student trained with
\cref{eq:eft} alone can therefore learn to detect a zoom from \emph{sharpness} instead of
geometry. The blur control of \cref{sec:audit} exposes exactly this (\cref{sec:eftresults}).
EFT+B adds a third term that removes the shortcut:
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}_{\text{B}} = \mathcal{L} + \mu\,\big\| \log \hat D_\theta(\mathcal{B}_{s'} I) - \log \hat D_0(I)\big\|_1,
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{B}_{s'}$ down-samples by an independent $s'\sim\operatorname{LogUniform}[1,3]$ and
up-samples back. A blurred image has the field of view of the original, so its target is the
unzoomed depth; detail loss alone is no longer evidence of a zoom. We use $\mu=1$.

\paragraph{Baselines and ablations.} Any fine-tuning changes a model, so improvements could come
from the training itself. An anchor-only run is not a valid control here: the student starts as
an exact copy of the teacher and sees the same input, so its loss and gradient are exactly zero
and it stays the frozen model (we verified this). The \emph{blur-only} control instead keeps the
anchor and the blur term and drops the zoom term, with the same images, steps, flips and
optimiser. We also report EFT+B with a three times stronger anchor ($\lambda=3$) to probe the
trade-off with accuracy on unzoomed images, and a variant in the style of Zhong \etal~\cite{zhong2023equivariance}
whose target is the student's own stop-gradient prediction on the unzoomed image instead of the frozen
teacher. All variants are scored with the same code.
