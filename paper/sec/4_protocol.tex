\section{The zoom audit}
\label{sec:audit}

\paragraph{Models.}
We test seven models in three groups (\cref{sec:related}):
\emph{pixels only} --- Depth Anything V2 metric Small and Base~\cite{yang2024dav2} (indoor
or outdoor head, matching the dataset) and ZoeDepth NYU+KITTI~\cite{bhat2023zoedepth};
\emph{predicts camera} --- Depth Pro~\cite{bochkovskii2025depthpro},
MoGe-2 ViT-S~\cite{wang2025moge2} and UniDepthV2 ViT-S~\cite{piccinelli2025unidepthv2};
\emph{takes camera} --- Metric3D v2 ViT-S~\cite{hu2024metric3dv2}, run once with the correct
zoomed $K'$ and once with the stale $K$.
All use the official weights and the authors' preprocessing (Hugging Face
\texttt{transformers} or the official repositories).

\paragraph{Data.}
KITTI depth val selection~\cite{geiger2013kitti,uhrig2017sparsity} (outdoor, LiDAR),
the 654 official NYUv2 test images~\cite{silberman2012nyu} (indoor, Kinect, Eigen crop),
and the DIODE validation set~\cite{vasiljevic2019diode} split into indoor and outdoor scans
(laser). From each of the four sets we draw a fixed random subset of 200 images (seed 0),
identical for all models. Ground-truth intrinsics come with all three datasets.

\paragraph{Zooms and controls.}
We zoom by $s\in\{1.25, 1.5, 2, 3\}$ about the image centre (bicubic, antialiased, as in a
phone's digital zoom). The zoom factor used in all fits is the exact one, $f'_x/f_x$ from
\cref{eq:law}, which absorbs the rounding of the crop box. The prediction on the zoomed image
is resampled onto the crop's own pixel grid by area averaging and compared, pixel by pixel,
with the prediction on the original image (label-free) and with ground truth (for accuracy).
To separate the camera change from the loss of detail, the \emph{blur control} down-samples
the image by $s=2$ and up-samples it again: same detail loss as a $2\times$ zoom, same field of
view. A subset of 50 images per set from KITTI and NYUv2 is also zoomed about the four
quadrant centres, where the principal point moves out of the image centre.

\paragraph{Optical versus digital zoom.}
A digital zoom changes the camera \emph{and} loses detail; a real (optical) zoom only changes the
camera. We separate the two with a resolution trick. Treat a $2\times$ down-sampled copy of each
image as the camera. Its \emph{optical} $2\times$ zoom is the same field of view cut from the
full-resolution image, with real detail; its \emph{digital} $2\times$ zoom is a crop of the
down-sampled image, up-sampled. Both have identical intrinsics and output size and are compared with
the down-sampled camera's own prediction on the same pixels. We also repeat the $2\times$ zoom with
bilinear instead of bicubic resampling, and compress the unzoomed image to JPEG quality 75.

\paragraph{Splitting a zoom into its causes.}
\label{par:cues}
A digital zoom changes two things at once. It throws away the context around the crop, and it makes
everything inside the crop $s$ times larger. To see which one moves the depth, we build two more images
from each photo (\cref{fig:cueexample}). \emph{Paste}: the $2\times$ crop stays at its own place and size,
and the rest of the frame is filled with the image's mean colour. The camera is unchanged, so the model
gets the original $K$, and only the context is gone. \emph{Shrink}: the whole image is made $2\times$
smaller and placed in the middle of a plain canvas, with its own intrinsics (the inverse of
\cref{eq:law}). Everything is still visible, only smaller. We then compare, on the same pixels, the paste
with the original (\emph{context} effect), the zoom with the paste (\emph{magnification} effect: same
content, $2\times$ larger), the zoom with the original, and the shrink with the original. A model that
respects the camera keeps depth in all four. A model that reads apparent size gives ratios of 1, 0.5, 0.5
and 2. The plain border is itself unusual, so we read the paste and shrink as interventions on the cue,
not as natural images. These runs use 100 images per set for the four fast models, and 50 for Depth Pro
and DA3-Metric-L.

\paragraph{Scope of the claim.}
For a model that does not receive the camera, a crop changes the visible context, and its output may
legitimately change where the content is ambiguous (\cref{sec:law}). The audit therefore measures how
far each model is from the law and in which direction; it is not a claim that every image-only
estimator can or should reach $\alpha=0$ on every image.

\paragraph{Metrics.}
\textbf{Label-free:} the exponents $\alpha$ and $\beta$ of \cref{eq:exponents}, and the depth
inconsistency at $2\times$, $\mathrm{DIE}_2=|\operatorname{median}_p \log
\hat D_2/\hat D_1|$. \textbf{With labels:} AbsRel and $\delta_1$ on the pixels of the crop
that have ground truth (capped at 80\,m for KITTI, 10\,m for NYUv2, 50\,m and 150\,m for
DIODE indoor and outdoor). We report medians over images with 95\% bootstrap intervals. Frames of one KITTI drive, one
DIODE scene or one NYUv2 scene are not independent, so the intervals in the main tables resample
whole scenes (cluster bootstrap); paired comparisons use Wilcoxon signed-rank tests with Holm
correction. We also report a shape error, the median absolute deviation of
$\log \hat D_s/\hat D_1$ from its median, which is zero when a zoom changes depth by a single global
factor.

\paragraph{Sanity check of the pipeline.}
Projecting random 3D points with $K$, applying the crop-and-resize to the pixel coordinates,
and projecting with $K'$ agree to $<10^{-3}$\,px for all zooms and crop centres, and a constant
depth map survives the zoom/unzoom round trip exactly.
